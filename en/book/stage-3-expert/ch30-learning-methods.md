# Ch 30 · Learning Methods in an Era of Rapid Change

> **Stage**: 尾章 · Closing | **Pillar**: 工程师修炼

## Core Question

How do you keep up when everything keeps changing?

## Soul Insight

Distinguish **paradigm shifts** from **tool replacements**. Only paradigm shifts require systematic re-learning. Most "new" things are new tools built on old paradigms — you should update your mental model incrementally, not restart from scratch. The engineer who masters this distinction learns faster than one who treats every framework as equally important.

## Concepts

- First-principles learning: start from "what problem does this solve?", not from the API
- The two types of change: new primitives (require re-learning) vs new abstractions over old primitives (require mapping)
- Building a personal knowledge system:
  - Input: books > papers > blog posts > documentation (in depth order)
  - Processing: Feynman technique — if you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it
  - Output: writing, teaching, building — forces synthesis
- The 20% that explains 80%: identify the core invariants of a domain before details

## Practical Strategies

- When encountering a new technology: read the motivation paper/post first, not the tutorial
- For a new language: write the same program you know well in it — contrast reveals the paradigm
- For staying current: follow researchers and practitioners who explain *why*, not just *what*
- For depth: one classic textbook in each domain beats ten blog posts
